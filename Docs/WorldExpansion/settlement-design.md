# Valley settlements

The expansion adds five deliberately planned settlements around the preserved starting home: Alderhaven, a regional city; Stonegate, an enclosed upland town; and the villages Brookmere, Reedbank and Highfield. Alderhaven has four residential wards, a civic quarter, a market square, tree-lined avenues and outer streets. Stonegate has a market court, masonry curtain walls, corner towers and four open gates. Village greens lead to small streets, orchards and cultivated plots; Highfield's wheat fields distinguish it from the garden villages.

`settlement-layout.json` is the reproducible layout contract. Positions are Unreal-world metres and represent ground contact, not the mesh pivot. The editor integrator subtracts each imported mesh's actual minimum Z after scaling. Cottage is 5.76 m wide and 5.76 m deep, Lodge about 5.2 m wide, and CivicHall about 8 m wide; buildings are placed close to authored scale. Imported FBX front faces local +Y, confirmed by the architecture import bounds compared with the Blender source.

The design reuses the admitted HomesteadPilot architecture, detailed stone modules, market stalls, lanterns, signs, trees, gardens and wheat. Existing mesh LODs and materials remain unchanged. There are 211 buildings across 225 individual architecture/market actors, plus 2,541 native instanced detail placements. These include 1,894 paving modules, 136 curtain-wall modules, 40 street/orchard trees, 240 gardens, 36 wheat modules and smaller planting/furniture. Regional vegetation is a separate terrain assignment.

The source generator checks that placements stay inside their flat pads and building centres remain separated. These checks establish layout consistency only. The integrator verifies the connected project and ValleyRegion map, reads native mesh bounds, places individual architecture, uses project-private FoliageTypes for repeated details, checks native instance-count deltas, and saves the level. Terrain supports walking under the thin decorative paving. Paving, crops and small details have no collision; walls and architecture retain collision. Original StartingHome actors and source mesh/material assets are not altered by the settlement scripts.

Main road arrival endpoints are cardinal: Alderhaven south `(300,138,24)` and west/east `(178,260,24)` / `(422,260,24)`; Stonegate south `(-320,296,35)` and west/east `(-410,390,35)` / `(-230,390,35)`. Village roads enter 62 m away from each centre along X or Y. Regional roads must meet those endpoints without crossing housing blocks diagonally.

Run `Scripts/WorldExpansion/settlement_layout.py` outside Unreal to regenerate the JSON. The coordinator runs `Scripts/WorldExpansion/settlement_integrate.py` in the verified editor after terrain import with the duplicated ValleyRegion map open and PIE stopped. The integrator can be rerun for the same manifest: it replaces exact manifest labels and only its own private foliage types. It does not remove obsolete actor labels from a different prior manifest; revisions that change labels require an explicit scoped migration.

Offline validation completed: Python compilation, pad-radius assertions and building-centre spacing. Editor integration, reopened persistence, visual inspection and CharacterMovement receipts are owned by the coordinator. This is a built environment expansion; settlement simulation, NPCs, quests, interiors for every building, streaming and a city-scale shipping performance budget are not implemented by this layout.

## Region collision refinement

The initial region integration exposed dense navigation-export warnings from the reused architecture's LOD0 collision. `settlement_collision.py` creates exact rendering copies of the six used architecture/market meshes under the region's own `Architecture/Meshes` package. It selects the existing authored LOD2 for complex collision, without generating solid boxes across doors or editing the original home. The script verifies three native LODs, descending triangle counts, retained render geometry/material bindings, and unchanged source package hashes. It then rebinds only the 225 exact settlement actor labels and preserves their transforms and collision profiles.

Native readback is recorded in `settlement-collision.json`; successful application writes `settlement-collision-assets.json`, which the integrator honors on subsequent runs. The integration receipt is updated to the final private mesh bindings. Reported triangle totals represent geometry selected for collision across placements, not a measured physics speedup. New cottage entry/exit, city streets, and navigation-warning behavior require editor testing after application.

## Additive urban dressing

The first city overview showed coherent houses but overly open lawns and weak public-space definition. `settlement_dress.py` addresses that with 24 near-unit-scale infill houses in unused avenue margins, 290 courtyard fence modules, 190 hedge modules, 62 garden plots, 22 avenue trees and 16 low moss-stone wall modules. Two additional market stalls and the existing authored detailed garden well strengthen the market square. The well's source is a real modeled masonry ring and lifting frame; its historical receipt marked visual review pending, so its new placement is subject to the coordinator's visual review.

This is a separate additive pass: 27 `WX_Dressing_` actors and 607 instances in private DressingFoliage types. It leaves previous settlement and home actors unchanged. Offline checks reject any new house whose conservative 6 by 6 metre envelope intersects an existing city street rectangle; they also enforce separation from existing structures and the city pad limits. Decorative fences/hedges do not add collision. Housing and stalls use the region's private LOD2-collision copies. `settlement-dressing-layout.json` records the intended placements; `settlement-dressing-integration.json` records native counts after application. The dressing script is rerunnable within its own prefix and foliage-type namespace.
